## External FAQ

### 1. Does Recovery Manager require cameras or image-processing hardware?
No. Recovery Manager is Step 5 in the Commerce Context pipeline and is deliberately headless. It operates purely on structured digital records and metadata generated by the first four operational Managers (Receiving, Prep, Pack, Returns). It references image URLs and content hashes collected upstream, but does not perform computer vision itself.

### 2. How does Recovery Manager determine whether a fee is illegitimate?
Recovery Manager evaluates each charge against a 4-way decision framework:
- **CONTRADICTED:** The upstream operational evidence proves the accusation is false (e.g., Amazon charges for a missing polybag, but Prep Manager logged a sealed polybag with a verified photograph and timestamp). The agent prepares a disputable claim.
- **SUPPORTED:** The upstream evidence confirms that a defect actually occurred in the warehouse. No claim is filed, and the seller is notified to correct internal operations.
- **SILENT:** No relevant operational records exist for the unit. In strict adherence to our core tenacy ("Do not invent evidence"), no claim is filed.
- **UNCERTAIN:** The evidence is ambiguous or conflicting (e.g., an unreadable barcode scan). The case is flagged for human review.

### 3. How does Recovery Manager protect our Amazon account from suspension?
Amazon strictly enforces policies against filing spam or fraudulent reimbursement claims. High claim denial rates trigger seller performance investigations. Recovery Manager protects your account by enforcing **Conservative Decision-Making**: claims are generated *only* when the evidence chain provides clear, verifiable contradiction. If the evidence is insufficient or uncertain, the agent declines to claim.

### 4. What types of Amazon fees can be disputed?
Recovery Manager currently handles five major fee dispute categories:
1. **Inbound Defect Fees:** Unplanned prep, missing polybags, suffocation warning defects, uncovered barcodes.
2. **Fulfillment Fee Weight Tier Overcharges:** Cubiscan measurement errors that bump standard items into higher weight tiers.
3. **Lost Inbound Inventory:** Units received at the dock that Amazon adjusts out of inventory without paying reimbursements.
4. **Unreturned Customer Refunds:** Buyer refunds where the physical unit was returned to stock or where 45 days elapsed without return.
5. **Duplicate Billings:** Erroneous duplicate charges for the same shipment or order.

## Internal FAQ (The Questions We'd Rather Not Answer)

### 1. What happens if upstream agents produce bad or hallucinated evidence?
**Answer:** Recovery Manager’s decisions are only as good as the evidence fed into it (Garbage In, Garbage Out). If Prep Manager erroneously logs `polybag_present_sealed=yes` when a unit was unbagged, Recovery Manager will generate a claim that Amazon’s fulfillment center will reject upon reviewing their receiving photo.  
*Mitigation:* Recovery Manager incorporates a **Kill Condition**: if Amazon’s dispute rejection rate exceeds 10% on filed claims, autonomous filing is immediately revoked, and the system alerts the warehouse management that upstream verification integrity has degraded.

### 2. What if Amazon refuses to accept photographs from our prep center?
**Answer:** Amazon Seller Support guidelines state that photos taken by third-party sellers prior to carrier pickup have varying weight depending on the support tier. In cases where Seller Support denies a photo-only claim, Recovery Manager falls back to cross-record correlation: combining carrier Bill of Lading (BOL) delivery receipts, certified scale logs from Pack Manager, and scan timestamps. Furthermore, we log every denial as structured training data to adapt our evidence threshold per dispute category.

### 3. Can two competing sellers access each other’s evidence if they use the same multi-tenant prep center?
**Answer:** Absolutely not. Rule 1 of our engineering contract dictates **Tenancy Isolation Before Any Feature**. Every database query, evidence join, and file lookup is strictly scoped to an authenticated `TenantContext`. All photo references use tenant-salted HMAC tokens, making guessable URL attacks across organizations computationally impossible. A tenant attempting to inspect another tenant’s records receives an immediate security exception.

### 4. How much does it cost to run the LLM claim drafting?
**Answer:** Exactly **$0.00**. Recovery Manager uses a hybrid architecture: 95% of data matching and classification is executed deterministically at microsecond latency. The remaining formal claim letter synthesis is executed in batches using the Google Gemini 2.0 Flash Free Tier (or our zero-cost deterministic fail-open legal synthesizer). This ensures prep centers operating at high unit volumes experience zero API cost overhead.

### 5. Why not file claims for every single ambiguous charge to see what Amazon pays?
**Answer:** Because in the real world, doing that gets the merchant's Amazon account terminated. Amazon’s Seller Code of Conduct explicitly prohibits submitting unsupported reimbursement claims. Recovery Manager is engineered for **defensibility over volume**. We would rather miss a marginal \$1.00 claim than file an unsupported claim that endangers a multi-million-dollar Amazon business.
